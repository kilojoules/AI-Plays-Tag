\begin{abstract}
In competitive self-play, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) dominates Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) by a 95-to-2 margin across 616 agents and six training paradigms---a gap that reward shaping, opponent diversity, and hyperparameter optimization cannot close.
We trace this dominance to a brief high-entropy bootstrapping phase: auto-tuned entropy temperature crashes from its initial value to near-zero within the first 10\% of training, and this transient window determines final agent strength.
Transplant experiments reveal that the advantage is encoded in the \emph{actor's} first hidden layer, not the critic: freezing the actor after bootstrapping produces stronger agents than continued training (57.9\% vs 38.9\% combined strength), and pairing the bootstrapped actor with a randomly initialized critic recovers 51.6\%.
The qualitative mechanism---a universal entropy decay schedule followed by actor feature crystallization---transfers from 2D tag to 3D MuJoCo Ant Sumo, though quantitative optima are environment-specific.
These findings invert the primacy bias narrative: in competitive settings, early features are not a liability to be periodically reset, but the most valuable thing the agent learns.
% NEW (Design C act) — added 2026-07; integrate or split into companion paper:
In a complementary pre-registered factorial study of PPO self-play, we find that reward shaping and opponent diversity are \emph{complements}, not substitutes ($\beta_{RA}=+2.05$ log-odds, $95\%$ CrI $[+0.72,+3.45]$): zoo training only helps shaped agents, dense shaping trades a $+30$pp mean improvement for a $3$--$4\times$ seed lottery, and mixed-reward control cells localize both effects to the \emph{seeker's own} reward rather than opponent quality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reward Shaping and Opponent Diversity Are Complements:\\A Pre-Registered Factorial Study}
\label{sec:designc}
% ============================================================
% NOTE (integration): this act studies PPO-only zoo self-play. Its SAC
% pilots collapsed under DEFAULT hyperparameters in the zoo protocol
% (HP-BROKEN gate), whereas sec:dominance reports SAC dominance in the
% cross-method gauntlet. These are different training protocols and HP
% regimes — reconcile explicitly in sec:discussion when writing it.

A natural follow-up to the null results of Section~\ref{sec:dominance}
is whether the two standard remedies for sparse-reward self-play ---
reward shaping \cite{ng1999shaping} and opponent diversity
\cite{bansal2018competitive,baker2020emergent} --- are substitutes:
does training against a zoo of past opponents deliver what dense
shaping delivers, making shaping unnecessary?
We pre-registered this question \cite{nosek2018prereg} as a $2\times2$
factorial in PPO \cite{schulman2017ppo} zoo self-play and answer it in
the negative --- and in the opposite direction: \textbf{shaping and
zoo training are complements}. Zoo training does nothing for
sparse-reward agents, dense shaping buys its large mean improvement at
the price of a $3$--$4\times$ between-seed variance inflation, and zoo
training is what tames that variance.

\subsection{Design, pre-registration, and evaluation}
\label{sec:designc-design}

Two reward presets bracket the shaping axis: \textsc{R4-sparse}
(terminal $\pm 10$ tag / $+6$ survival only, both roles) and
\textsc{R7-kitchen-sink} (eight dense terms: seeker pursuit gradient
and escalating urgency; hider distance, wall-proximity, minimum-speed
and survival terms; a both-roles area-coverage bonus).
Zoo intensity $A\in\{0,0.5\}$ is the probability that the seeker's
rollout opponent is a uniformly sampled past snapshot of its
co-evolving hider rather than the live hider.
All runs: 5M steps, HSM $=1.15$, \texttt{four\_corners} layout,
identical PPO hyperparameters.
The design, decision thresholds, and analysis model were pre-registered
before data collection (v1--v2) with a pre-registered power extension
to $n{=}20$ seeds per cell (v3, frozen before launch, fixed-$n$, no
interim fits); 195 training runs total.
Following Section~\ref{sec:env}'s lesson that within-run metrics
mislead --- every \textsc{R7} seeker reaches 88--96\% training win rate
against its own hider while spanning 27--99\% against a common
population --- all outcomes are population evals
\cite{balduzzi2018reevaluating}: a $32\times32$ cross-pool gauntlet
(99{,}200 episodes) and a fixed 4-anchor panel ($175$ runs $\times$ 5
opponents $\times$ 30 episodes).

The confirmatory estimand is the reward$\times$zoo interaction
$\beta_{RA}$ in a Bayesian logistic mixed-effects model (NUTS;
Binomial per (run, anchor) counts; random intercepts per run and per
anchor; $\sigma_{\text{run}}$ stratified by reward), with a
pre-registered PURSUE/REFINE/KILL rule at
$|\beta_{RA}|\geq\log 1.25$ and 95\% CrI excluding zero.

\subsection{The seed lottery: variance is the phenomenon}
\label{sec:designc-variance}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\columnwidth]{figures/design_c_taxonomy.png}
\caption{Failure-mode taxonomy over 175 runs: anchor-panel mean vs.\
own-hider win rate. \emph{Basins} (both low) never learn pursuit;
\emph{over-specializers} (own $\geq 0.8$, panel $<0.5$) beat their
co-evolved partner and lose broadly. Sparse-reward failures are
basins; dense-reward failures are over-specializers; zoo dose clears
basins at $A\geq 0.1$ and thins over-specialization $\sim3\times$ by
$A{=}0.5$.}
\label{fig:designc-taxonomy}
\end{figure}

The headline interaction is inseparable from a second, arguably more
consequential finding: $\sigma_{\text{run}}[\textsc{R7}] /
\sigma_{\text{run}}[\textsc{R4}] \approx 3$ (log-odds; CrIs
non-overlapping, Table~\ref{tab:designc-confirm}) --- \textbf{dense
shaping trades mean for variance}, turning the random seed into a
lottery over policy quality (gauntlet range $0.27$--$1.00$ across
\textsc{R7} seeds vs.\ $0.20$--$0.43$ for \textsc{R4}).
Classifying every run by (panel, own-hider) win rate
(Figure~\ref{fig:designc-taxonomy}) resolves the lottery into two
discrete failure modes with different dose-response to $A$:
\textsc{R4} fails by \emph{basin} (14/20 runs never learn; no zoo dose
rescues them --- $\beta_A\approx0$), while \textsc{R7} fails mostly by
\emph{over-specialization} (6/20 at $A{=}0$).
An $A$-dose sweep ($A\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5\}$) shows basins vanish
by $A\geq0.1$ while over-specialization shrinks monotonically but
retains a tail ($2/20$ even at $A{=}0.5$): a small zoo dose breaks the
never-learn equilibrium; un-narrowing an already-specialized policy
takes sustained opponent diversity.
Checkpoint diagnostics rule out forgetting: a permutation-null test
finds $0/29$ runs with above-null forgetting regret --- failed runs
learn the wrong thing or nothing, they do not lose what they had.

Ablating individual \textsc{R7} terms at $A{=}0$ implicates the
shaping terms themselves as the variance source: removing the
area-coverage bonus yields $15/15$ healthy runs (cell mean $0.82$,
the best of any cell), and removing both coverage and urgency
collapses between-run $\sigma$ from $1.7$--$2.1$ to $0.95$.

\subsection{Deconfound: the effects live in the seeker's reward}
\label{sec:designc-deconfound}

Because seeker and hider co-train in one run, the ``reward'' factor
above changes \emph{both} roles' rewards --- every
\textsc{R4}-vs-\textsc{R7} contrast is also an opponent-quality
contrast, and a skeptic can attribute both headline effects to the
opponent: a stale sparse-reward zoo hider is a useless curriculum.
Mixed-reward control cells break the collinearity:
\textsc{R7sk-R4hd} keeps \textsc{R7}'s seeker-side terms (coverage
excluded) while the hider receives exactly \textsc{R4}'s terminal-only
reward ($A\in\{0,0.5\}\times10$ seeds).
Against the \emph{same sparse hider}, seeker-side shaping alone
reproduces the main effect ($+1.94$ $[+0.81,+3.21]$, certified), the
interaction's direction and magnitude ($+1.48$ $[-0.21,+3.20]$,
$P{=}0.96$ at $n{=}10$/cell), and the variance inflation
($\sigma_{\text{run}}=1.95$ vs.\ $0.66$); toggling \emph{hider-side}
shaping at fixed seeker shaping produces no interaction ($-0.02$
$[-2.35,+2.32]$) and identical $\sigma_{\text{run}}$.
\textbf{Complementarity and the seed lottery are properties of the
agent's own reward, not of its opponent's competence.}
One refinement emerges: over-specialization re-appears \emph{without}
the coverage bonus when the hider is sparse ($3/10$) --- partner
narrowness enables over-fitting; coverage amplifies it.

\subsection{Mechanism: exploration variance, not strategic diversity}
\label{sec:designc-mechanism}

Two mechanisms could produce \textsc{R7}'s wide quality distribution:
rich reward admits \emph{multiple distinct strong strategies}
(strategic diversity), or a single strong strategy that only some
seeds \emph{find} (exploration variance).
We pre-declared a discriminating test: behavioral fingerprints (six
role-correct trajectory features vs.\ the 4-anchor panel, PCA) either
align with the outcome skill axis (exploration variance) or cluster
away from it (strategic diversity).
The result is unambiguous:
$|\mathrm{corr}(\text{behavior PC1}, \text{SVD } U_1)| = 0.81$,
above the pre-declared $0.7$ bar --- strong seekers occupy one region
of behavior space, and the lottery is over \emph{finding} it.
Consistent with this, the cross-pool win matrix is dominated by a
single transitive axis (61\% of centered variance) with low-energy
cyclic residuals --- the ``weakly non-transitive'' spinning-top regime
\cite{czarnecki2020spinning}.

\subsection{Robustness, errata, and disclosure}
\label{sec:designc-robust}

An adversarial code-and-methods review of this study (three
independent audit passes) found and we disclose: (i) the gauntlet's
adaptive episode-escalation thresholds were mathematically
unreachable, so all matchups uniformly ran 100 episodes --- benign
(the unweighted per-episode models assume exactly this) but the
methods claim was corrected; (ii) a GAE done-mask off-by-one
\cite{schulman2016gae} affected \emph{all} runs equally --- same-seed
reruns with the corrected estimator reproduce the gap and the
variance (bug immaterial); (iii) two early refine rounds deviated
from the pre-registered schedule and the heteroscedastic
$\sigma_{\text{run}}$ term was a post-hoc addition --- the v3
extension re-certified the interaction under a properly frozen,
fixed-$n$ protocol, and the variance finding replicated out-of-sample
on new seeds and a second evaluation population; (iv) a role-indexing
bug invalidated early behavioral features --- all behavioral results
above use the corrected pipeline.
Variational-Bayes intervals were $10$--$12\times$ narrower than NUTS
on the same models and would have flipped one verdict; only NUTS
intervals support claims.
The failure taxonomy is not an artifact of the speed ratio: at
HSM $\in\{1.05, 1.20\}$ collapse persists and scales monotonically
with hider speed ($0.74/0.63/0.57$ cross win rate; $1/8$, $3/8$,
$5/8$ runs below $0.5$).
SAC could not be included: under default hyperparameters in this zoo
protocol both SAC pilots collapsed (peak $55\%\to14\%$;
$29\%\to8.7\%$ by 10M steps, entropy temperature $\to0.001$),
tripping the pre-registered HP-BROKEN gate; the algorithm-asymmetry
question remains open (cf.\ Section~\ref{sec:dominance}).

\paragraph{Practical guidance.}
For self-play practitioners: (1) shaping and opponent diversity are
not interchangeable --- budget for both, because shaping without
diversity buys a seed lottery and diversity without shaping buys
nothing; (2) evaluate against populations, never against the training
partner or any single opponent; (3) a small zoo dose ($A\approx0.1$)
is enough to eliminate never-learn basins, but over-specialization
needs sustained diversity; (4) audit both-roles reward terms
(coverage-style bonuses) first --- they are over-specialization traps.
